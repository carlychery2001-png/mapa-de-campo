

# AREA: visual-system

I measured the bundled build in a 360x640 and a 320x568 viewport. On a small phone the map controls overlap each other. Idle, the Layers and Import buttons sit right on top of the zoom buttons. During navigation the card takes 326 to 363 px of height and pushes 2 or 3 buttons off-screen or under the top bar. On Android the web viewport is also smaller than the device, because the WebView is inset by the status and navigation bars. So the 320x568 case is the common one. Other key text gets clipped: the Northing in the coordinate bar is cut off, and the GPS chip is pushed past the screen edge while a track is recording. Night mode only changes the panels: the GPS chip, north, zoom and side buttons, scale bar, notes and lightbar stay white or pastel, and night borders (1.32:1) are invisible. Seven side buttons have icons only. Several icons are reused for different actions (pin 7 times, route 5, sliders icon for both 'Rename' and 'Change CRS') or break conventions ('+' means Import). There is no prefers-color-scheme, reduced-motion or focus styling. Most buttons give no pressed feedback because the tap highlight is turned off. The CSS uses 17 font sizes and 12 corner radii, with a second off-brand blue (#0a84ff). The planned direction is right with two changes. First, cut the side buttons to 3–4 and add a labelled bottom bar instead of putting labels on 7 round buttons. Second, keep coordinates with a '.' decimal in every language so they can be copied into a GIS; format only distances and dates by locale.

# AREA: main-flows

The core field features are solid: offline GeoTIFF, follow-line, stake-out, track recording, waypoints and the planner, with careful error handling. The main screen and panels are still built for one place and one kind of user, though: an expert at EARTH University in Costa Rica. The main problems are seven icon-only floating buttons that collide with the zoom and north controls on ordinary phones, and no i18n layer (every string is inline Spanish, including Java toasts). Costa Rica defaults leak everywhere: CRTM05 preselected in the CRS dialog, es-CR dates, Google tiles set to es-419/CR, EARTH demo data and ~65 MB of orthophotos auto-installed for every user. Jargon (Track, SRC, GeoTIFF, EPSG, XYZ, GNSS, elipsoide) appears on the first screens, and the welcome is a one-shot text wall that also suggests a simulation that fails. The token/theme, labeled-tools, guided-intro and multi-language direction is right, with three additions: (1) a first-run 'where will you work?' choice that gates the EARTH pack and the CRTM05/UTM defaults, (2) wider CRS support read from the file itself, or worldwide users will see their data in the wrong place, (3) honour the Android font size and add unit choice (metric/imperial). Proposed IA: a bottom bar of 4 labeled buttons plus 'Más' (Capas, Marcar, Grabar, Medir, Más), a large Mi ubicación button, zoom made optional, the north arrow only when rotated, and a grouped menu.

# AREA: editor

The editor works and has good foundations: autosave with retry, undo/redo, hints for each tool, a confirm before discarding a drawing, and visible text labels on the tools. As a product for "anyone on earth" it falls short in five ways. (1) The bottom bar shows 10 to 14 tools at 64 px each in a horizontal scroller with its scrollbar hidden, so on a 360 px phone "Más" and "Terminar" are off-screen, and "Listo" and "Terminar" share the same check icon. (2) The labels lean on GIS terms ("Vértices", "Ajuste", "Polígono", "capas vectoriales", "px", "elipsoide", references to QGIS). (3) Several field-safety gaps: a GPS point is saved with no accuracy check, GPS averaging cannot be cancelled and can lose its result, the only undo after a delete is a toast you cannot tap, the selection colour is almost the same magenta as the default layer colour, and the edit handles are small for gloves. (4) The data itself is Spanish: attribute keys and values like fecha, fuente:'mapa' and 'GPS promediado 10 s', a boolean parser that only knows sí/no, and field names that must be Latin letters. (5) Every message is built by concatenating Spanish strings with "(s)" plurals and gendered articles, and Costa Rica-only systems (CRTM05, SingularPad, es-CR dates, CR placeholders) are always shown. On the planned direction: drop a generic first-run carousel in favour of contextual, first-use help for each tool inside the edit bar. Keep data keys and values language-neutral and translate only how they are displayed. Pin the most important actions (undo, more, done) outside the scroller and show selection tools only once something is selected. Suggested tool names (Spanish / English): Seleccionar/Select · Punto/Point · Línea/Line · Área/Area · Editar forma/Reshape · Mover/Move · Borrar/Delete · Imán/Snap · Deshacer/Undo · Más/More · Guardar y salir/Save & exit. While drawing: Deshacer punto/Undo point · Agregar mi posición/Add my position · Grabar caminando/Record as I walk · Terminar línea or Terminar área/Finish line or Finish area · Descartar/Discard.

# AREA: research

The leading field apps (Esri Field Maps, Mergin Maps, QField, Avenza, Gaia GPS) share one layout: a full-screen map, a few always-visible primary actions (Add/Record, Layers, Locate), a GPS accuracy readout, and everything else under a "More" menu. Point and line capture works from a centre crosshair with add / undo-last / save / cancel buttons. Material 3 now marks the bottom app bar as deprecated in favour of docked or floating toolbars. Its guidance sets a 48dp/8dp touch-target floor, a type scale, and colour roles with medium and high contrast variants. For bright sun, studies favour dark text on a light background, so the default daytime theme should be a light, high-contrast one with a dark theme as an option. The WCAG 2.2 AA items that matter here are 4.5:1 text contrast, 3:1 contrast for controls and icons, 24px minimum targets (map pins fall under the Essential exception), text resizable to 200% (pass the Android font scale to setTextZoom instead of locking it at 100), and reflow for panels. To publish worldwide, the app must target API 36 (deadline was 31 Aug 2026). It needs location permission requested in context, a location-type foreground service for recording (and a prominent disclosure if background location is used), a privacy policy and Data safety form even for offline-only apps, and a new precise-location declaration that becomes mandatory on 27 Jan 2027. For localization, follow the device language with an in-app override, pass the locale to the WebView, and use Intl.PluralRules and Intl.NumberFormat. Accept decimal commas through type=text with inputmode=decimal, never build sentences by joining strings, and leave room for short labels to double in length.

- [high/layout] Main map screen: overall structure — Leading field apps converge on one pattern. Mergin Maps: map with Sync, Add (Record), Layers and More; GPS button bottom-left showing accuracy coloured against a threshold; location button bottom-right that centres and follows until the user pans (https://merginmaps.com/docs/howto/input_ui). Esri Field Maps: Layers, Search and an overflow menu (basemap, bookmarks, legend, measure, markup); a location banner with accuracy and provider; a location button cycling show / centre north-up / centre heading, grey when unavailable; Add; a swipe-up panel for feature details (https://doc.arcgis.com/en/field-maps/android/use-maps/quick-reference.htm). Avenza's toolbar has GPS/compass, Add placemark, Layers and Map tools, and tracking was promoted onto the toolbar (https://store.avenza.com/blogs/avenza-maps/explore-the-record-gps-tool-in-the-avenza-maps-app). Gaia GPS: Record in the top stats bar, a Layers icon, and a Plus button for waypoint, route or area (https://help.gaiagps.com/hc/en-us/articles/115003524567).
  → Full-bleed map. Bottom docked toolbar with 4-5 labelled tools: Layers, Mark, Record, Measure, More. More holds Planner, Editor, Import/Export and Settings. A separate Locate button sits bottom-end and cycles off / centre / follow+heading, with a colour-coded accuracy badge (for example green ≤ 5 m, amber ≤ 15 m, red beyond; user-configurable). A compact status strip at the top shows GPS accuracy and, while recording, time, distance and point count. Measure, markup and basemap belong in tools or overflow, not as permanent buttons.
- [high/clarity] Mark point / draw line or polygon / editor — QField captures with a centre crosshair. '+' adds a vertex, '-' removes the last one, Save finishes (minimum 2 vertices for a line, 3 for a polygon) and an X button cancels (https://docs.qfield.org/how-to/data-collection/digitize/). Mergin Maps adds a streaming mode that drops vertices by time or distance interval from GPS (https://merginmaps.com/docs/howto/input_ui).
  → Use the crosshair model, which works with gloves because no precise tap is needed. Show one contextual action bar with big labelled buttons: Add point here, Add at GPS, Undo last, Finish, Cancel. Disable Finish until the minimum vertex count is reached and say why. Offer 'Stream from GPS' with time and distance settings. Show a live vertex count, length and area.
- [high/clarity] Track recording control — QField track recording has a time interval, a distance interval and a 'maximum tolerated distance' that throws out bad GNSS jumps, and a badge marks the layer that is tracking (https://docs.qfield.org/how-to/navigation-and-positioning/tracking/). Its start and stop sit behind a long-press, which is hard to discover. Gaia keeps Record visible in a stats bar.
  → Make Record a visible labelled button. While active, show a persistent red 'Recording' state with elapsed time, distance and points, plus Pause and Stop. Stop asks for confirmation and saves automatically. Show the same state in the Android foreground-service notification. Expose interval and outlier-filter settings under 'Advanced' with sensible defaults. Do not hide primary actions behind long-press or double-tap.
- [high/onboarding] First run / empty state — In NN/g's study, people who read deck-of-cards tutorials did no better (91% task success) than people who skipped them (94%), were not faster, and rated the app harder to use. NN/g recommends contextual help instead (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-tutorials/). Android permission guidance says to ask in context, never block, and degrade gracefully (https://developer.android.com/training/permissions/requesting).
  → No mandatory tutorial. The first screen is an empty state with one large 'Open map file (GeoTIFF/GPX/KML...)' button and a 'Try sample map' button. Show one-time coach marks only the first time a tool opens, with Skip. Request location when the user first taps Locate, Mark-at-GPS or Record, not at launch. Keep Help in More, with short illustrated how-tos.
- [high/accessibility] All touch controls (glove use) — Google's minimum is 48x48dp (about 9 mm; recommended range 7-10 mm) with at least 8dp between targets (https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/7101858). Gloves need more. MIL-STD-1472 is cited as 15 mm square bare-handed and 20 mm square gloved, with 3 mm gaps (secondhand, via Ray et al., https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edsair,edsair.doi...........9465c900e5780072de7a168d106fd14d). Parhi et al. give about 9.2-9.6 mm for bare thumbs (https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=155150). In a WebView with width=device-width and initial-scale=1, 1 CSS px is about 1 dp.
  → Floor: 48 CSS px targets and 8 px gaps everywhere. Primary field actions (Record, Mark, Locate, Finish/Undo): at least 56-64 px. Add a 'Large controls (gloves)' setting that scales controls by about 1.4-1.6x (72-96 px, roughly 12-15 mm; M3's large FAB is 96dp) with gaps of 12 px or more. Provide on-screen +/- zoom and rotate-reset buttons so pinch and two-finger gestures are never required.
- [high/accessibility] Target size compliance (WCAG 2.5.8 AA) — WCAG 2.5.8 requires at least 24x24 CSS px. Exceptions: spacing, an equivalent control, inline, user-agent control, essential. Map pins are explicitly covered by Essential because their position carries meaning, but authors are 'strongly encouraged' to offer an alternative (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html).
  → All chrome (buttons, list rows, sheet handles) meets 48 px, well above the 24 px floor. Dense map points are acceptable, but give them a larger invisible hit radius (about 24 px), a disambiguation list when several fall inside it, and a feature list in the Layers sheet as the equivalent route.
- [high/visual] Outdoor / sunlight readability — Studies consistently show better legibility for dark text on a light background, linked to display luminance and smaller pupils (Piepenbrock, Mayr & Buchner 2014, https://docserv.uni-duesseldorf.de/servlets/DerivateServlet/Derivate-31413). Dobres et al. 2017 looked at ambient light and polarity in glance-like reading (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28166901/). Sunlight raises the screen's black level and reflections hurt light-coloured UI most (https://grayhill.com/blog/how-sunlight-readability-testing-ensures-clear-reliable-visual-interfaces/). No formal standard for outdoor contrast was found; practitioners treat WCAG as the floor and aim for AAA.
  → Default daytime theme: light, high contrast. Near-black text (#000 to #1a1a1a) on white or very light surfaces, and solid opaque toolbars with no translucent glass over imagery. Design targets: at least 7:1 for text and at least 4.5:1 for icons and control outlines, beyond the 4.5:1 and 3:1 WCAG floors. Avoid light greys, pastels and thin 1px strokes. Labels drawn over the map or imagery get a 2-3 px halo. Keep dark mode as an option for dawn, dusk and night. Test on real phones in direct sun at about 50% brightness.
- [high/accessibility] Contrast (WCAG 1.4.3 / 1.4.11) — Android guidance: 4.5:1 for small text, 3:1 for large text (18pt regular or 14pt bold), and icons should meet the same ratios (https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/7158390). WCAG 1.4.11: 3:1 for the visual cues that identify a control, its state (selected, on) and its focus indicator, with 3:1 as a hard threshold; disabled controls and essential imagery are exempt (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html).
  → Check every token pair in all four themes with a contrast checker, and add an automated check to the build. Active-tool, recording-on and toggle states need at least 3:1 against neighbouring colours and a second cue (fill plus label or icon change). Give focus rings at least 3:1, 2-3 px wide.
- [high/accessibility] Android font scale → WebView (WCAG 1.4.4) — WCAG 1.4.4: text must resize to 200% with no loss of content (failure F69: clipped or overlapped text) (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/resize-text.html). Android 14+ font scaling goes up to 200% with a nonlinear curve (https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/features). By default the WebView scales text by the system font scale, and setTextZoom() overrides it (Chromium AwSettings, https://crrev.com/f311a84728272e30979432e8474089b3db3c67df/android_webview/java/src/org/chromium/android_webview/AwSettings.java; https://developer.android.com/reference/android/webkit/WebSettings#setTextZoom(int)). setTextZoom enlarges text but not fixed box sizes.
  → Never call setTextZoom(100) to lock text size. Either leave the default, or set it to round(resources.configuration.fontScale*100) and update it in onConfigurationChanged. Optionally add an in-app text size slider (100-200%) that combines with the system value. Use min-height, not fixed height, and let toolbar labels wrap to two lines. Test at 200% on the toolbar, sheets and dialogs.
- [high/layout] Edge-to-edge insets and back navigation (WebView shell) — For targetSdk 36 on Android 16, apps cannot opt out of edge-to-edge, and the back key no longer reaches onBackPressed (KEYCODE_BACK is not dispatched) (https://developer.android.com/about/versions/16/behavior-changes-16). Third-party reports say env(safe-area-inset-*) returns 0 or wrong values in WebView before version 140 (https://dev.to/capawesome/capacitor-edge-to-edge-safe-areas-the-complete-guide-k30); not verified against Chromium release notes.
  → Read WindowInsets natively and inject them as CSS variables (--inset-top/bottom/left/right), with env() as the fallback, so the toolbar and FAB clear the gesture bar and camera cutout. Use OnBackPressedCallback / OnBackInvokedCallback to forward Back to JS, closing the open sheet, then cancelling the active tool, then exiting.
- [high/onboarding] Play release: target API — Since 31 Aug 2026, new apps and updates must target Android 16 (API 36) (https://developer.android.com/google/play/requirements/target-sdk).
  → Set targetSdk 36 and complete the edge-to-edge, predictive-back and large-screen work above before submitting.
- [high/error-message] Location permission flow — Android: ask in context, show a rationale when shouldShowRequestPermissionRationale() is true, offer a cancel option, and degrade gracefully. Do not nag, do not block the app with full-screen warnings, and do not push users to Settings to reverse their choice. 'Only this time' grants exist on Android 11+ (https://developer.android.com/training/permissions/requesting).
  → The map, layers, measure and planner must work without location. If location is denied, show the Locate button in an error state and an inline message where GPS data would appear ('Location off: GPS features unavailable'), with a small 'Enable' link only when the user taps that feature. If the user grants approximate rather than precise location, show a clear 'Approximate location: accuracy about 1-3 km' warning on the GPS badge and block survey-grade actions.
- [high/onboarding] Background recording: foreground service and background location — Location foreground service: declare FOREGROUND_SERVICE and FOREGROUND_SERVICE_LOCATION, use foregroundServiceType="location", and pass FOREGROUND_SERVICE_TYPE_LOCATION. Location permission must be granted before startForeground(), and the service cannot start while the app is in the background without ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION (https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/changes/fgs-types-required). Play policy: the service must start 'as a continuation of an in-app, user-initiated action' and stop when the task ends. Service use equivalent to background location falls under the background rules: Play Console declaration, a video of about 30 s, and a prominent disclosure (https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9799150). Foreground service types must also be declared under App content (https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13392821).
  → Avoid ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION. Start the location foreground service only when the user taps 'Start recording' (or starts route following) while the app is visible, show an ongoing notification with Stop, and stop the service on Stop or Finish. Declare the 'location' service type in Play Console with a short video of the user starting a recording.
- [high/wording] Prominent disclosure dialog (if any location use happens outside the visible app) — The disclosure must appear in-app during normal use, in a dialog before the runtime prompt, not only in the privacy policy. It must use the word 'location', a background phrase ('when the app is closed or not in use') and list the features. Google's template: '<App> collects location data to enable <feature> even when the app is closed or not in use.' (https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9799150). It must explain why, what and how in language a 13-year-old understands, offer grant and decline buttons ('Not now'), and not imitate system UI (https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/11150561).
  → Write the disclosure once and translate it into every shipped language. Example: 'Field Map collects location data to record your track and follow routes, even when the app is closed or not in use, while a recording is running. Location stays on this device unless you export it.' Buttons: 'Agree' / 'Not now'. Use the app's own styling.

  → Fill in the precise-location declaration now under 'Live tracking': continuous precise GNSS for track recording, route following, stakeout and area measurement. List each user-facing feature. Plan the location-button flow for one-off 'mark at my position' before moving to targetSdk 37.
- [high/onboarding] Privacy policy and Data safety — Every Play app must complete Data safety and link a privacy policy, including apps that collect nothing. Collection means data leaving the device, including through the app's own WebViews and SDKs. Processing only on the device does not count. Precise location is an area under 3 km² (https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469). A privacy policy is required both in Play Console and inside the app (https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/11150561).
  → For a fully offline app: declare 'no data collected or shared', confirm no analytics or crash SDK, and that the WebView loads no remote resources (fonts, CDNs, tiles). - [high/i18n] Language selection and WebView locale — Android 13+ offers a per-app language setting (LocaleManager or AppCompatDelegate.setApplicationLocales). A locale change triggers a configuration change or activity restart (https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/LocaleManager). - [high/i18n] String catalog, placeholders and plurals — Android: no hardcoded strings, a complete default (fallback) string set, comments giving translators context and the space available, and placeholders marked with xliff:g and positional arguments (%1$s) (https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/resources/localization). Intl.PluralRules returns zero/one/two/few/many/other: English uses two of these, Arabic all six. It has been widely available since 2019 (https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/JavaScript/Reference/Global_Objects/Intl/PluralRules).

Choose plural variants with new Intl.PluralRules(locale).select(n). Add a translator note for each key giving its maximum width. - [medium/layout] Layers panel — Field Maps: layer visibility, errors and filters. QField legend options: visibility, labels, opacity, zoom to layer, feature list (https://docs.qfield.org/how-to/qfield-interface/map-interaction/). Gaia: checkmarks plus an opacity slider.

- [medium/consistency] Toolbar / FAB component choice (Material 3) — Material 3 Expressive deprecates the bottom app bar ('still available, but not recommended') in favour of the docked toolbar, which can embed the primary action instead of using a FAB and moves extra items into overflow (https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/components/DockedToolbar.md). - [medium/visual] Color system / theming tokens — Material 3 colour roles are Primary, Secondary and Tertiary (each with On-, Container and On-Container variants), Error, Surface with Surface Container Lowest to Highest, Surface Dim/Bright, Outline, Outline Variant and Inverse Surface.   → Define CSS custom properties named after the M3 roles (--md-primary, --md-on-primary, --md-surface-container-high, --md-outline...). Pass the system contrast level and dark-mode flag from native code to the WebView and switch themes from them, with an in-app override. Reserve red for recording and errors, and always pair colour with an icon or label.
- [medium/visual] Typography scale — M3 type scale in sp: Display 57/45/36, Headline 32/28/24, Title 22/16/14, Body 16/14/12, Label 14/12/11 (https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/theming/Typography.md). - [medium/layout] Panels, sheets, dialogs (WCAG 1.4.10 Reflow; Android 16 large screens) — WCAG 1.4.10: content must fit 320 CSS px wide without two-dimensional scrolling. Maps are exempt, but the panels around them are not (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html). For targetSdk 36, Android 16 ignores orientation and resizability locks on screens with smallest width ≥ 600dp (https://developer.android.com/about/versions/16/behavior-changes-16).
  → Sheets and dialogs use single-column, vertically scrolling layouts that work from 320 px up. Support landscape and tablets: a side panel instead of a bottom sheet at 600 px or wider, and a vertical floating toolbar in landscape. Save map position and in-progress drawings across rotation and configuration changes.
- [medium/i18n] Numeric input (coordinates, distances, grid spacing) — input type=number has inconsistent decimal-comma behaviour: the keyboard can show '.' while the locale expects ','. Number() and parseFloat() return NaN for '3,5', so valid input fails validation for comma-locale users (https://medium.com/justeattakeaway-tech/solving-cross-browsers-localization-on-numeric-inputs-3f7aec57aaeb; https://lists.webkit.org/pipermail/webkit-unassigned/2022-October/1068106.html). Intl.NumberFormat.formatToParts exposes the locale's 'decimal' and 'group' symbols (https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/JavaScript/Reference/Global_Objects/Intl/NumberFormat/formatToParts).
  → Use <input type="text" inputmode="decimal"> with a parser that reads the decimal and group symbols from formatToParts, accepts both ',' and '.' as decimal, strips group separators and validates. Show the parsed value back in locale format. For coordinate fields, also accept DMS and signed decimal degrees, and show what was understood ('9.8765° N').
- [medium/i18n] Button labels and layout under translation — IBM figures cited by W3C: English strings of up to 10 characters can grow 200-300% in translation, 11-20 characters 180-200%, 21-30 characters 160-180%, and over 70 characters about 130%. Short labels grow most. Avoid tight fixed widths and space-saving abbreviations, and allow taller line height for Thai, Arabic and CJK (https://www.w3.org/International/articles/article-text-size). Apps targeting Android 16 can no longer use the compact 'UI fonts' for Arabic, Thai, Tamil and others, so layouts must handle taller glyphs (https://developer.android.com/about/versions/16/behavior-changes-16).
  → Toolbar labels: allow two lines, keep icons, and do not truncate silently. Buttons size to their content with min-width. Test with a long locale (German or Finnish, not only French or Portuguese), an RTL locale (Arabic) and a tall-script locale (Thai or Hindi). Use CSS logical properties (margin-inline-start, inset-inline-end) and dir=rtl. Mirror only directional UI icons (back, undo), never the map, compass or north arrow.
- [low/wording] Microcopy across the app — Android recommends a short, friendly style and standard terminology, which also makes translation easier (https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/resources/localization). Play asks for disclosure text a 13-year-old can read (https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/11150561).
  → Use verbs for action labels ('Record', 'Mark point', 'Measure'). Avoid GIS jargon in primary UI: 'Layers', not 'TOC'; 'Accuracy ±4 m', not 'HDOP'. Keep technical terms for an Advanced section. Error messages say what happened and what to do ('No GPS signal yet. Move to open sky and wait about 30 s.').
